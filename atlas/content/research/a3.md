## Result

**In the mid-1930s the public share was far higher than in 1952.** Neither HOLC nor FNMA existed before 1933, so the share is set to zero for 1925–1932. The Home Owners' Loan Corporation, created in June 1933, refinanced distressed home mortgages, and by 1935 HOLC and FNMA held 14.1% of household mortgages and consumer credit; scaled to Z.1's definitions the estimate is 15.0% (see Splice). As HOLC ran off and total debt swung, the share fell in every later year except 1942, to 5.0% in 1944. In Z.1 it was 3.51% in 1945, a low of 1.62% in 1947, and 3.16% in 1952, where the quarterly series begins.

| Year | Public share | Source | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1925–1932 | set to zero (no HOLC or FNMA yet) | Historical Statistics | C |
| 1935 | 14.1% (15.0% scaled to Z.1) | Historical Statistics (HOLC, FNMA) | B |
| 1945 | 3.51% | Z.1 annual | A |
| 1947 | 1.62% | Z.1 annual | A |
| 1952 | 3.16% | Z.1 annual = Z.1 quarterly at 1952Q4 | A |

Reliability: **A** = Fed Z.1. **B** = federal holdings from HOLC and FNMA reports, total debt from Grebler, Blank and Winnick via *Historical Statistics*, which warns that these estimates "must be used with caution". **C** = no HOLC or FNMA yet, so the federal share is set to zero; other public holders are not measured in the source.
