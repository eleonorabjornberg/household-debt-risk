# A8 definition crosswalk (draft, unverified)

> **Claude draft, not the author's text.** A starting map of what "public" or "guaranteed" could mean in each system. **Every row is unverified**: it is written from general knowledge, because none of the national sources could be reached (`AVAILABILITY.md`). Each cell must be checked against the national source named before it is used, and no figure appears here until then.

| | Who bears the credit loss on a typical mortgage | What could count as "public" | National source to check (blocked today) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US** (project baseline) | Lender or investor, unless the loan sits with the GSEs, in Ginnie pools (FHA/VA/USDA) or with a public holder | Held by federal, GSE/agency or state and local holders (Z.1 holder view, as in the rest of the project) | Fed Z.1 (have) |
| **Canada** | Lender, but high-ratio loans must carry mortgage default insurance; CMHC's insurance carries a full federal backing, private insurers' a partial one | (a) CMHC-insured balances; (b) all insured balances (government backstop); (c) NHA MBS and Canada Mortgage Bonds outstanding (a funding guarantee, not the same as credit insurance) | CMHC annual report and mortgage-insurance data; OSFI; Bank of Canada; StatCan |
| **Denmark** | Mortgage banks (realkredit) keep the credit risk on their balance sheet and fund it with covered bonds; no general state guarantee | Little or nothing on a holder or guarantee basis; covered bonds are private. Possible edge cases: crisis-era guarantees | Danmarks Nationalbank, Statbank DK (realkredit lending), Finanstilsynet |
| **UK** | Lenders (banks, building societies) | Government schemes only: Help to Buy equity loans (government is a junior lender), the Mortgage Guarantee Scheme (partial guarantee to lenders), student loans (government lender) | Bank of England mortgage statistics; gov.uk scheme statistics; Student Loans Company |
| **Germany** | Banks, savings banks, Bausparkassen | Public-law banks (Sparkassen, Landesbanken) are publicly owned but not a state guarantee of the borrower's loan; KfW promotional loans are a federal development bank's lending | Bundesbank lending statistics; KfW annual report |

**The comparability problem.** The US measure is a *holder* measure (who owns the loan or the guaranteed pool). Canada's main public exposure is *insurance* on loans held privately. Germany's largest public-law lenders are publicly *owned*, which is neither. One metric will not fit all five; that is Q5 in `tracks/analysis.md`.
